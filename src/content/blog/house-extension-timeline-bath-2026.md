"How long will it take?" is the second question every Bath homeowner asks when planning an extension (the first is always "how much?"). It is also one of the hardest questions to answer honestly, because the timeline is affected by planning permission, party wall agreements, building regulations, structural complexity, weather, and the availability of your builder.

This guide breaks down what to realistically expect for a house extension in Bath in 2026 — based on actual project durations across Bathampton, Batheaston, Widcombe, Oldfield Park, Larkhall, Bathford, Combe Down, Bear Flat, and Weston over the last 18 months.

## Typical House Extension Timelines in Bath

### Single-Storey Rear Extension — 12 to 18 weeks on site

The most common extension in Bath. A single-storey rear extension on a Victorian or Edwardian terrace typically takes 12-18 weeks of construction, plus 8-12 weeks of pre-construction (design, planning, building regulations). End-to-end: roughly 5-7 months from initial enquiry to handover.

**What is included in the timeline:**
- 2-4 weeks: Design, measured survey, initial drawings
- 2-3 weeks: Planning permission determination (or 48 hours for permitted development notification)
- 1-2 weeks: Building regulations drawings and structural calculations
- 1-2 weeks: Party wall agreements (if applicable)
- 1 week: Site setup, scaffolding, groundworks
- 8-14 weeks: Construction
- 1-2 weeks: Snagging, decoration, handover

### Double-Storey Extension — 16 to 26 weeks on site

Substantially more complex. The structural work takes longer, the building regulations sign-off is more involved, and there is more second-fix work (staircase, two sets of electrics, two sets of plumbing, more decoration). End-to-end: 7-10 months.

### Side-Return Extension — 10 to 16 weeks on site

Common in Bath's Victorian terraces, where the narrow side return is filled in to widen the kitchen. Less structural work than a full rear extension, but access is usually more difficult. End-to-end: 4-6 months.

### Wraparound Extension (side + rear combined) — 16 to 24 weeks on site

The biggest of the conventional extensions. Combines the complexities of both side-return and rear extension work. End-to-end: 7-9 months.

## What Affects the Timeline in Bath

**Planning permission in Bath is slower than most places.** Bath & North East Somerset Council has a statutory determination period of 8 weeks for most householder planning applications, but conservation area and World Heritage Site applications routinely take longer. If you are in the central Bath conservation area, budget 12-16 weeks for planning. If your extension is in the World Heritage Site buffer zone and you need a heritage statement, budget 16-20 weeks.

**Permitted Development removes 6-8 weeks if you qualify.** A single-storey rear extension under 4 metres (or 6 metres for a terraced or semi-detached house, under the larger PD rights) does not need full planning permission, but does need a prior approval — [our planning permission guide](/blog/house-extension-planning-permission-bath/) explains when full consent kicks in submission. The determination is 8 weeks but is usually a formality.

**Party wall agreements add 2-4 weeks to most Bath extensions.** The Party Wall etc. Act 1996 requires you to serve notice on your neighbours if your extension falls within 3 metres of their boundary or involves work to a shared (party) wall. The notice period is 2 months for new walls on the boundary, 1 month for work to an existing party wall. Neighbours can dissent, which triggers a formal party wall agreement and can add 4-8 weeks to the programme.

**Building regulations add 1-2 weeks for the design, then run in parallel with construction.** A full plans building regulations application takes 5-8 weeks to determine. A building notice is faster (48 hours) but cannot start until 48 hours after submission. For most extensions, full plans is the right approach — it gives you a proper set of drawings to price from, and avoids surprises on site.

**Bath's housing stock adds time.** Georgian and Victorian properties often have unexpected surprises behind the plaster — old chimneys not in use, redundant pipework, hidden structural issues. Always budget a 2-week contingency for any extension in an older Bath property.

**Access and logistics.** If your extension requires materials to be hand-carried up a steep Bath hill, down a narrow alley, or through the house (because there is no rear access), allow 1-2 weeks of additional programme time.

## How to Speed Up a Bath Extension

**1. Get your drawings right before you start pricing.** A builder pricing from sketch drawings cannot give you a fixed price, and you will end up with variations on site. Invest in proper measured survey drawings and a full set of building regulations drawings before asking for quotes.

**2. Sort party wall early.** Talk to your neighbours as soon as you have decided to extend. The party wall process is much faster when neighbours are informed and onside. A friendly conversation over the garden wall can save 4-6 weeks of formal agreement.

**3. Choose a builder who is not booked 6 months out.** A builder booked out 6 months is not necessarily the best builder — they may just be good at marketing. A good Bath extension builder typically has 2-3 months of forward work, which means they can start your project soon after planning is granted.

**4. Lock the design before starting on site.** Changes during construction are the single biggest cause of programme overrun. If you decide halfway through that you want a different window, or the bifold door in a different position, expect to add 2-4 weeks and several thousand pounds to the project. Get the design right first, build it once.

## Common Causes of Programme Overrun in Bath

**Underground services.** A surprising number of Bath extensions are delayed because the builder hits an unexpected drain, a redundant gas main, or a BT cable that is not where the records say it is. A proper ground radar survey before starting reduces (but does not eliminate) this risk.

**Weather.** A wet Bath winter can put 2-4 weeks onto a project. Foundations cannot be poured in heavy rain, roof timbers cannot be exposed in driving rain, and external render cannot be applied below 5°C. Choose a builder who plans for the weather rather than pretending it does not happen.

**Late materials.** A Bath extension in 2026 typically uses 4-6 weeks of lead time for the windows, bifold doors, and sometimes the steel beam. A builder who orders everything on day one of the project, rather than when each element is needed, will not run out of materials halfway through.

**Building control delays.** Building control inspections have to be booked in advance, and a busy Bath building control team can have 1-2 week lead times for inspections. A builder who sequences the work to book inspections early avoids waiting for the inspector.

## What a Realistic Programme Should Look Like

A good Bath extension builder will provide a written programme before starting on site. It should include:

- All major work stages (groundworks, structure, roofing, first fix, second fix, decoration, snagging)
- Target dates for each stage
- Lead time for major materials
- Inspection dates for building control
- Float between stages (typically 1-2 weeks of programme contingency)
- A clear handover date

If your builder cannot or will not provide this, ask why. Most reputable Bath extension builders have a written programme as standard.
