A new kitchen is one of the biggest home improvements a Bath homeowner will ever commission. It is also one of the easiest places to overspend, and one of the hardest to compare quotes on — because every kitchen fitter prices differently, and the difference between a basic refit and a full reconfiguration with structural work can be £15,000 or more on the same property.

Here is an honest breakdown of what you should expect to pay for a kitchen refit in Bath and BANES in 2026.

## Kitchen Refit Types and Typical Costs

### Like-for-Like Kitchen Refit — £8,000 to £15,000

A straightforward replacement of the existing kitchen in the same footprint. Same plumbing positions, same electrical points, no walls moving. Remove the old kitchen, install the new one, tile the splashback, decorate. Works well when the existing layout already functions and you simply want new units, worktops and appliances.

### Kitchen Refit with New Layout — £15,000 to £28,000

More common in Bath's older housing stock where the existing kitchen is small, poorly positioned or opens onto a dining room you would rather combine. The fitter reroutes plumbing and electrics, may add or remove a wall (non-load-bearing), and installs a new kitchen in a different footprint. This is where you start to see real design value — and where quotes start to vary significantly between fitters.

### Kitchen Refit with Structural Work — £28,000 to £45,000+

For when you want to remove a load-bearing wall, install an RSJ, or knock through into a rear extension. Requires a structural engineer, building regulations sign-off, and coordination with any other work happening in the house. The kitchen itself is often only a third of the total cost — the structural work and the re-plaster/redecorate after the wall comes down are where the rest of the budget goes.

## What Drives the Price in Bath Specifically

**Bath's housing stock is varied, and the kitchen rarely dictates the budget.** A Georgian townhouse kitchen in Bathwick, a Victorian terrace in Oldfield Park, a 1930s semi in Bear Flat and a 1970s detached in Weston will each throw up different problems. Narrow doorways, low ceilings, uneven floors and chimney breasts all need to be worked around. The more your property deviates from a standard rectangular room, the more your kitchen will cost.

**The Bath plumbing and electrical infrastructure is often older than the kitchen it serves.** Many Bath properties still have partial original wiring, lead pipework, and fuse boxes that need upgrading before a new kitchen can be safely installed. A good quote will allow for this; a bad one will not mention it and you will get a variation order on day three of the job.

**Bath stone and period features can be your friend or your enemy.** An exposed Bath stone wall is a beautiful backdrop to a kitchen — but it also needs sealing before tiling, may have lime mortar behind the plaster that needs careful handling, and may sit within a conservation area that restricts external alterations (such as new soil pipes). Always mention period features when getting a quote.

**Access is a real cost driver in Bath.** Terraced streets with no rear vehicle access, narrow alleyways between houses, and Bath's hills all add to the time it takes to get materials in and waste out. If your kitchen fitter has to hand-carry a full kitchen up four flights of stone steps, that cost is in your quote somewhere.

## What a Fixed-Price Quote Should Include

A proper kitchen refit quote from a Bath kitchen fitter should itemise:

- Removal and disposal of the existing kitchen
- Rerouting of plumbing to new positions
- Rerouting of electrical circuits to new positions
- New electrical points (sockets, switches, appliance circuits)
- Plastering and making-good after the rerouting
- Supply and installation of new kitchen units
- Worktop templating and installation
- Splashback tiling
- Flooring (if included)
- Appliance installation
- Decoration to affected areas
- Building regulations notification (for new electrical circuits and structural work)
- VAT

If a quote does not break these down, ask for the breakdown before signing.

## How to Compare Bath Kitchen Quotes

Quotes are hard to compare because no two fitters price the same way. Three things to check:

**1. What is included vs excluded?** A £12,000 quote that excludes tiling, flooring, appliances and decoration is not the same as a £18,000 quote that includes everything. Get the full list of inclusions and exclusions in writing.

**2. Who is supplying the kitchen?** If the quote includes supply-and-fit, check the kitchen brand, range and specification. A Howdens Wickes or B&Q kitchen at trade prices is very different from a Magnet or a bespoke joinery kitchen. The same kitchen name on a quote can mean different things to different fitters.

**3. Is the quote fixed-price or estimated?** A fixed-price quote is a contract — the price does not change unless you change the scope. An estimate is a guess. For a kitchen refit over £10,000, always insist on fixed-price.

## Common Pitfalls in Bath Kitchen Refits

**The cheapest quote is rarely the best.** Kitchen fitters who quote significantly below the going rate usually either exclude items they will add later, or are working without proper insurance, contracts or building regulations compliance. The cost of fixing a kitchen that has been wired incorrectly or plumbed without proper pressure testing is far higher than the saving on the original quote.

**Provisional sums are not the same as fixed prices.** A provisional sum is a placeholder for something the fitter has not priced properly. The final cost of the provisional sum can be 20-40% higher than the original allowance. Ask for as few provisional sums as possible, and for any unavoidable ones, ask what assumptions have been made.

**"While we are in there" extras.** Many kitchen fitters use the disruption of a refit as an opportunity to upsell extra work. Sometimes this is legitimate — you do need to redecorate, and yes, the floor underneath the old kitchen probably does need replacing. Sometimes it is upselling. Get a second opinion before agreeing to any significant extras.
